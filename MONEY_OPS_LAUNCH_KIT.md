## Mission and positioning

Aim: **A small, trustworthy online store that tests whether useful, low-risk products can sell profitably in the UK.**
The long-term dream can be saving toward personal goals in 2027, but an online shop must start with customer value, compliance and unit economics, not dreams of guaranteed passive income.

**Prioritised niche for research:** cable management and home-workspace accessories.
Potential examples (not verified supplier offers):
- Reusable cable ties and desk cable clips, sold individually or as a tidy desk bundle.
- Mechanical laptop/desk organisation items (non-electric).
- Reusable travel packing organisers (separate alternative niche).
Avoid untested electronics, power products, kids' safety products, cosmetics, supplements, trademarked copies, counterfeit products and unsupported medical claims.

**Brand example:** Orbit Tidy — _More room for what matters._ Working brand name only: search UKIPO, domains and marketplaces before adopting.

### Promise to customers

> Less desk clutter. More room to think. Thoughtful, practical organising accessories with clear delivery times, real images and straightforward returns.

No fake reviews, fake scarcity or made-up ratings. Product photos must be owned/licensed, and descriptions must match the items actually shipped.

## Stage 0: basic due diligence — before spending anything

1. Research demand: 20 comparable listings, buyer complaints, observed delivery times and realistic UK prices. Record dates and URLs.
2. Shortlist 3 suppliers. Confirm they can ship legally and reliably to UK buyers; get physical samples delivered to yourself.
3. Obtain exact total landed unit cost: product, VAT/duties where applicable, postage, packaging and payment fees.
4. Verify supplier address, commercial terms, stock, returns address, refund process and full product specifications.
5. Test the customer experience: order receipt, tracking, packaging, condition on arrival, usability and returns.
6. Verify trademark, domain, business registration and tax obligations before trading.
7. Pick *one* hero product or genuine bundle. Do not publish 100 random dropship products.

**Supplier email — draft only, not automatically sent:**
> Hello, I am exploring a small UK-based online store for desk-organisation accessories. Could you confirm your price for 1, 20 and 100 units, delivery timeline to UK customers, returns arrangements, stock updates, tracking, packaging options, applicable taxes and documentation? I would like to order a sample before discussing sales. Thanks.

## Stage 1: unit economics (scenario, not forecast)

Example:
- Sale price paid by customer: £29.99
- Cost of product and UK fulfilment: £11.50 (illustration only)
- Payment fee: 2.9% of sale + £0.30 (illustration; confirm platform)
- Customer service / expected returns allowance: £1.50
- Advertising cost per acquired order (CPA): £6.00
- Other per-order variable overhead: £0.50

Approximate contribution per order:
£29.99 − £11.50 − (0.029×£29.99+£0.30) − £1.50 − £6.00 − £0.50 = **£9.32** (before fixed costs, income tax and additional taxes).

These are hypothetical numbers and do not establish customer demand. If the shop is VAT-registered or must charge VAT, model VAT properly; use accounting advice. Include chargebacks, damaged parcels and refunds in real costs.

**Hard go/no-go rule**: do not buy ads if you don't know item cost, delivered cost and return policy. If measured CPA exceeds break-even CPA, stop and investigate rather than scaling losses.

## Legal checklist for UK selling

- GOV.UK distance selling: tell buyers about their rights, including a 14-day cancellation period for most online orders after delivery (exceptions exist). https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses
- UK return/refund law: https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds
- CAP/ASA: don't mislead about product features, prices, testimonials, demand or delivery. https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-and-resources/advice-for-businesses.html
- ICO/PECR: marketing emails/DMs require an appropriate lawful basis and commonly consent; no unsolicited mass messages or fake followers. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/
- Tax, business setup, insurance, product safety, VAT and import requirements: verify for your specific products. Seek professional advice if unsure.

## What can be automated *after* explicit platform connection

**Permitted, useful automation proposals**:
- Draft a weekly *private* report from your own store's real orders and costs.
- Alert when inventory is low, when returns spike or when advertising exceeds the approved limit.
- Create draft descriptions/images using real product data, for manual approval.
- Generate an opt-in newsletter **draft** for customers who consented.

**Not included**:
- Spam bots, mass unsolicited DMs, fake testimonials, traffic or reviews
- Purchasing products, starting ads, charging accounts or messaging customers without approval
- Fake earnings reports, false guarantees or illegal evasion of marketplace policies
